A Primer on Markov Chains

Background
A Markov chain is a special type of dynamic system that evolves
probabilistically.
The defining characteristic of Markov chains is that they have no
memory.
They are really useful in modelling things like the evolution of the
weather from day to day.
They are also finding new application is modelling and
characterising network behaviour. We are interested in this latter
application of Markov chains.

Background
The operation of a Markov chain describes a process where a
system can be in a finite number of states.
For example, the weather can be ”hot”, ”cold”, ”rainy”, ”icy”, and
so on.
The transition from one state to another is probabilistic, rather
than deterministic. For example, if its ”rainy” today, we can say
with probability p that it will be rainy tomorrow.
R
Tomorrows weather cannot be determined with certainty, but we
can assign probabilities to the various possible weather conditions
conditioned on todays weather.

Example
The weather in a certain city is ”sunny”, ”cloudy” or ”rainy”. If it
is sunny one day then sun or clouds are equally likely the next day.
If it is cloudy there is a fifty percent chance of sun, a twenty five
percent chance of continued clouds, and a twenty five percent
change of rain.
If it is raining, it will not be sunny, but continued rain or clouds are
equally likely.
Draw the state transition diagram representing this chain.

Finite Markov Chains
A finite Markov chain is a discrete time process that at any step
can be in a finite number of states.
As each step of the chain, the chain may change from one state to
the other.
The change from one state to the other is determined
probabilistically, according to a set of transition probabilities.
A chain where these probabilities do not change in time is called a
homogeneous or stationary chain.

and other related questions.

Regular Markov chains
The simplest transition matrices are associated with regular Markov
chains (sometimes called irreducible and primitive Markov chains).
Definition
A Markov chain is said to be regular if there exists a finite m such
that Pm > 0 (all entries are positive).
Note that not all chains are regular, but many important ones are.
However, the theory is a little more elegant for regular chains.
The main theorem for regular chains is stated in the next slide.

Regular Markov chains
Theorem
Let P be the transition matrix of a regular chain. Then.
(i) Corresponding to the matrix P, there is a single eigenvalue
λ = 1. All other eigenvalues are smaller in magnitude than
this eigenvalue.
(ii) There is a unique positive vector such that p T = p TP.
(iii) The limit of Pm as m → ∞ is a rank-1 matrix whose rows are
equal to p.
